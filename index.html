Light Stats is designed to respect your privacy from the ground up. This policy is short because there is almost nothing to disclose.

Data Collection

Light Stats does not collect, transmit, or store any personal data. All system metrics are read locally and displayed directly on your device. No information ever leaves your Mac.

The app reads the following system-level information solely for display purposes:

  • CPU usage percentage and per-core utilization
  • GPU usage percentage (supported Macs only)
  • Memory pressure, used, and available memory
  • Disk capacity and available space
  • Network upload and download throughput
  • Fan speed in RPM (supported Macs only)
  • List of running applications (Memory Cleanup tab only)

Local Processing

All data processing happens on your device. The app uses macOS system APIs — Mach, IOKit, SMC — to read hardware metrics. No data is cached to disk beyond what the system already tracks.

Network Access

Light Stats makes no network requests. It does not include analytics, crash reporting, ad frameworks, or any third-party SDKs that communicate over the network. The app's network entitlements are limited to reading interface statistics locally.

Third Parties

Light Stats does not integrate with any third-party services, analytics providers, or advertising networks. There are no third parties to disclose.

Open Source

The full source code is available on GitHub. You can inspect exactly what the app does and how it works.

Changes

If this policy ever changes, the update will be reflected here and noted in the app's release notes. The app will never silently change its data handling behavior.

Frequently asked

How do I change what shows in the menu bar?
Click the ellipsis (…) button in the popover to open Settings. Toggle individual items under Status Bar Items.
Why do some items show "N/A"?
GPU and Fan metrics require supported hardware. Apple Silicon Macs without fans, or Macs without a discrete GPU, will show N/A for those readings.
Does the app affect battery life?
The default refresh rate is 2 seconds. At this interval, CPU overhead is negligible (typically under 0.5%). You can lower the refresh rate in Settings if preferred.
Is force-quitting apps safe?
Force quit bypasses an app's normal shutdown and may cause data loss for unsaved work. The app shows a confirmation dialog before force-quitting. Use normal quit when possible.
